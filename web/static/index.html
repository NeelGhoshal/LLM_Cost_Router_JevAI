Let the small model answer. Call the big one only when the answer looks wrong.

A cheap open model answers first. Jev reads the question and the answer and estimates the chance the answer is wrong. At or above your threshold, the prompt goes to a larger, slower and dearer model instead.

    What the evaluation found

    Measured offline on 200 held-out benchmark prompts (MMLU, MMLU-Pro, ARC-Challenge, GSM8K) with qwen3:1.7b as the small model and qwen3:8b as the large one. The live demo above uses a different, hosted pair, so its thresholds are untuned starting points.

    0.927
    AUROC for Jev telling the small model's wrong answers from its right ones.
    41% less time
    for the speed-oriented cascade than always using the large model, at 73.0% accuracy against 80.0%.
    No saving in dollars
    for this pair: one Jev check costs about 31% of a large-model call, and the small model is right only 58% of the time.
    Pre-routing lost
    to random routing. The prompts Jev rates hardest are ones the large model also gets wrong.
